# Composition rules

- Default article canvas 1600×900. Social 1200×630 and Pinterest 1000×1500 are opt-in. Respect explicitly requested dimensions rather than forcing historical assets into the default.
- Start with about 5% safe outer margins (80px on a 1600px canvas). Keep meaningful labels away from crop edges. Native-aspect rendering is the current card convention; inspect any proposed crop separately.
- One editorial message, one main subject. Use product names or a short headline; keep supporting copy in HTML. Do not paste the full article deck into the image.
- Favor large names/headlines that survive a 320px-wide thumbnail. Tiny category labels are optional; no essential qualification may exist only in tiny text.
- Wrap exact text without rewriting product names. Reduce clutter before reducing type size. Inspect actual font rendering, long names, apostrophes and non-ASCII characters.
- Use meaningful geometry and alignment. An arrow asserts order or causation: omit it for unordered decisions. A check mark asserts a judgment: use only when supported and bounded. Avoid stars, numerical ratings, winner badges, earnings, savings or awards without documented evidence and approval.
- Workflow simplification must retain consequential review/consent/approval gates. A cover may summarize; captions must not be used to conceal a false claim in the artwork itself.
- Keep diagrams explicitly editorial. Synthetic panels are not product screenshots or test evidence. Do not invent product UI, integrations or proprietary marks.
- Supplied artwork can be used within the authorized scope. Flag a misleading label before integration; propose a concrete corrected brief rather than silently rewriting the article to fit the image.
